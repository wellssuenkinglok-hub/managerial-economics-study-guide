Chapter 1 — Introduction to Managerial Accounting

Learning Objectives

  • Distinguishing features of managerial vs. financial accounting
  • The three broad functions of management (Planning, Directing, Controlling)
  • Organizational structure (shareholders, board, CEO, CFO, controllers/treasurers/internal auditors)
  • Business ethics & corporate social responsibility (triple bottom line)

Key Formulas & Rules

  • Financial accounting = external users, historical, verifiable, whole company, must follow IFRS/ASPE.
  • Managerial accounting = internal users, future-focused, relevant & timely, segments of the org, no required format.
  • Planning → Directing → Controlling: the three functions every manager performs.
  • Controllers keep the books & internal controls; Treasurers manage cash; Internal auditors check reliability of both.
  • Triple bottom line = People, Planet, Profit.

Chapter 2 — Managerial Cost Concepts & Cost Behaviour

Learning Objectives

  • 3 classes of manufacturing cost (DM, DL, MOH); product vs. period costs
  • Variable, fixed, and mixed costs; relevant range
  • High-low method to split mixed costs
  • Cost of Goods Manufactured (COGM) and Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) schedules

Key Formulas & Rules

  • Total cost of a unit = Direct Materials + Direct Labour + Manufacturing Overhead
  • Product costs = DM + DL + MOH (attach to inventory). Period costs = selling & admin costs (expensed when incurred).
  • High-Low: VC/unit = (Cost@High − Cost@Low) ÷ (Activity@High − Activity@Low)
  • FC = Total cost at either point − (VC/unit × activity at that point) [must match at both points]
  • TC = VC(q) + FC
  • Direct Materials used = Opening RM + Purchases − Ending RM
  • COGM = Opening WIP + DM used + DL + MOH − Ending WIP
  • COGS = Opening FG + COGM − Ending FG

iClicker 6–8 Q6
Maintenance costs for a factory:
Jan: 4,000 MH, $18,000 | Feb: 7,500 MH, $26,000 | Mar: 5,200 MH, $21,000
Apr: 8,000 MH, $25,000 | May: 3,000 MH, $16,500 | Jun: 6,000 MH, $23,000

6) What is the VARIABLE cost per machine hour?
A. $1.70 B. $1.90 C. $2.11 D. $3.30
7) What is total FIXED cost?
8) What is total cost at 12,000 machine hours?
Show answer & explanation

Answer: 6) A — $1.70/MH 7) $11,400 8) $31,800

Chapter 3 — Job Order Cost Accounting

Learning Objectives

  • Purpose & flow of costs in a job-order system
  • Job cost sheets: DM + DL + MOH applied
  • Predetermined overhead rate (PDOHR): calculate & apply
  • Under- vs. over-applied manufacturing overhead

Key Formulas & Rules

  • Cost of a job = Direct Materials + Direct Labour + Manufacturing Overhead APPLIED
  • PDOHR = Estimated total overhead for the period ÷ Estimated activity (DLH, MH, DL$, units, etc.)
  • Applied MOH = PDOHR × ACTUAL activity for the job/period
  • Actual MOH > Applied MOH → Underapplied (increase COGS)
  • Actual MOH < Applied MOH → Overapplied (decrease COGS)
  • RM → WIP → FG → COGS: each formula's output feeds the next stage's input.

Chapter 5 — Activity-Based Costing (ABC)

Learning Objectives

  • Traditional costing vs. ABC
  • 4 steps of ABC: activities → drivers → activity rate → allocate
  • Value-added vs. non-value-added activities
  • When ABC helps (and its limitations)

Key Formulas & Rules

  • Step 1: Identify activities & their estimated overhead cost pools.
  • Step 2: Identify a cost driver for each activity (the thing that best explains why the cost occurs).
  • Step 3: Activity rate = Estimated overhead for the activity ÷ Expected use of the cost driver.
  • Step 4: Allocate = Activity rate × actual driver usage by the product/job/department.
  • Traditional costing = ONE overhead rate/base for everything. ABC = MULTIPLE rates, one per activity — more accurate when products differ a lot in volume/complexity.

Practice Questions (8)

Answer: A) Units produced B) Direct labour hours C) Machine hours

Chapter 6 — Cost-Volume-Profit (CVP) Analysis

Learning Objectives

  • Contribution margin ($, per unit, ratio)
  • Break-even point (units & sales $) and target net income
  • Sales mix & weighted-average CM/CM ratio for multi-product break-even

Key Formulas & Rules

  • Contribution Margin (CM) = Sales − Variable Costs
  • CM per unit = Selling Price/unit − Variable Cost/unit
  • CM ratio = CM ÷ Sales
  • Net Income = Sales − Variable Costs − Fixed Costs (at break-even, NI = 0)
  • Break-even units = (Fixed Costs + Target NI) ÷ CM per unit
  • Break-even sales $ = (Fixed Costs + Target NI) ÷ CM ratio
  • Sales mix (multi-product): Weighted CM = Σ(CM per unit × sales mix %); Weighted CM ratio = Σ(CM ratio × sales mix %)
  • Multi-product BE units = (FC+Target NI) ÷ Weighted CM, then split by sales mix % per product.

Chapter 10 — Budgeting & Budgetary Planning

Learning Objectives

  • Purpose of a master budget & its components
  • Sales, production, direct materials, direct labour, MOH, S&A budgets
  • Budgeted income statement
  • Cash budget (receipts, disbursements, financing)

Key Formulas & Rules

  • Sales Budget: Sales $ = Expected volume × Selling price (first budget prepared).
  • Production Budget: Required production = Budgeted sales (units) + Desired ending inventory − Beginning inventory.
  • Direct Materials Budget: DM needed for production = Units to produce × DM per unit. DM purchases = DM needed + Desired ending DM − Beginning DM. Cost = purchases × cost/unit of DM.
  • Direct Labour Budget: Total DL cost = Units to produce × DL hours/unit × DL rate/hour.
  • Manufacturing OH Budget = Variable OH (rate × DLH) + Fixed OH (flat amount); keep variable & fixed separate.
  • Cash Budget = Beginning cash + Cash receipts − Cash disbursements ± Financing = Ending cash.

Chapter 11 — Budgetary Control & Responsibility Accounting

Learning Objectives

  • Static budgets vs. flexible budgets
  • 4 steps to build a flexible budget
  • Favourable (F) vs. Unfavourable (U) variances
  • Responsibility accounting basics (cost/profit/investment centres)

Key Formulas & Rules

  • Static budget = ONE level of activity, set before the period starts.
  • Flexible budget = budgeted RATES applied to the ACTUAL level of activity (a 'redo' of the budget at what actually happened).
  • 4 steps: 1) Split costs into variable/fixed. 2) Set a budgeted rate for each variable cost (rate = static $ ÷ static activity). 3) Apply rate × actual activity (fixed costs unchanged). 4) Compare flexible budget to actual → mark Favourable/Unfavourable.
  • Actual costs > Budget (or Actual income < Budget) = UNFAVOURABLE (U). Actual costs < Budget (or Actual income > Budget) = FAVOURABLE (F).

Chapter 12 — Standard Costing & Variance Analysis

Learning Objectives

  • Standard (budgeted) vs. actual costs
  • Materials price & quantity variances
  • Labour price(rate) & quantity(efficiency) variances
  • Variable overhead rate & quantity(efficiency) variances

Key Formulas & Rules

  • Glossary: AH=Actual Hours, SH=Standard Hours, AP=Actual Price, SP=Standard Price, AQ=Actual Quantity, SQ=Standard Quantity, AR=Actual Rate, SR=Standard Rate.
  • Materials Price Variance (MPV) = AQ × (AP − SP)
  • Materials Quantity Variance (MQV) = SP × (AQ − SQ)
  • Labour Price/Rate Variance (LPV) = AH × (AR − SR)
  • Labour Quantity/Efficiency Variance (LQV) = SR × (AH − SH)
  • Variable OH Rate Variance (VORV) = AH × (AR − SR)
  • Variable OH Quantity/Efficiency Variance (VOQV) = SR × (AH − SH)
  • A POSITIVE result (Actual > Standard) = Unfavourable (U). A NEGATIVE result (Actual < Standard) = Favourable (F).
  • Total variance for a cost = Price/Rate variance + Quantity/Efficiency variance.
